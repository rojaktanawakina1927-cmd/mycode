# Shot Plan — The Belt That Buys You Time

**Format:** 1080x1920, 30 fps, 1,847 frames (61.57 s)
**Audio:** `voice.wav`, 48 kHz mono, `en-US-AndrewNeural`, rate +15%, 222 words
**Final render:** 1440x2560, `--scale 1.3333`
**Style:** semi-realistic medical mannequin in a dark car interior; translucent skeleton; cyan force vectors; warm ivory bone highlights; no blood, wounds, or real crash victims.

## Global rules

- The mannequin is neutral, adult, and non-branded. No facial detail; matte graphite skin with a simplified ivory skeleton.
- Use a mannequin, not a photoreal patient. Anatomy is intentionally simplified and not to scale.
- No captions or subtitles. Only four short 3D-anchored labels are allowed: `PELVIS`, `RIBS`, `SHOULDER`, `HIP BONE`.
- Keep the camera locked or on a slow controlled move. No handheld shake.
- Impact uses 0.25x slow motion, then returns to real time. No flying glass, no screaming, no sirens.
- Music sits under the voice; SFX are restrained. Per skill mix: music +3 dB, SFX -6 dB, voice unchanged.
- The video ends on the belt and the spoken takeaway, not on an on-screen text card.

## Anatomy and physics notes

- Show a simplified rib cage, pelvis, and shoulder girdle. Do not show organs in clinical detail.
- The lap belt must sit low across the pelvic bone, not the abdomen. The shoulder belt crosses the chest and collarbone, away from the neck.
- Force arrows point inward and spread across bone, not into soft tissue. Keep arrow direction consistent between shots.
- The force limiter is a small mechanical detail inside the retractor or buckle stalk; do not imply the webbing tears.
- The “unbelted ghost” is a translucent path overlay, not a second person. It fades before any impact with the dashboard.
- No claim that a belt prevents all injuries. The script says it reduces risk and manages force.
