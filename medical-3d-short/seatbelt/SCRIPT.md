## Facts and sources

- Seat belts reduce serious crash-related injuries and deaths by about half. — CDC, *Facts About Seat Belt Use* (updated Jan 27, 2026): https://www.cdc.gov/seat-belts/facts/
- Lap and shoulder belts reduce the risk of fatal injury by 45% in cars and 60% in SUVs, vans, and pickups. Moderate-to-critical injury risk drops by about 50% in cars and 65% in SUVs, vans, and pickups. — IIHS, *Seat belts* (updated July 2026): https://www.iihs.org/topics/seat-belts
- An unbelted occupant keeps moving at travel speed until hitting the vehicle interior; belts let occupants “ride down” the crash and spread forces across the shoulder, rib cage, and pelvis. — IIHS, same page.
- Belts also prevent ejection, which is associated with a much higher risk of death. — IIHS, same page.
- Proper fit: lap belt low across the hips and pelvic bone, not the stomach; shoulder belt across the chest, away from the neck; never under the arm or behind the back. — Utah Department of Transportation, *Click It Utah*: https://clickit.utah.gov/seat-belts-adults-and-teens/
- Global context: about 1.16 million people die in road traffic crashes each year; road traffic injuries are the leading cause of death for ages 5–29. — WHO, *Road traffic injuries* (July 20, 2026): https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries

**Accuracy note:** the video uses a mannequin and simplified anatomy. It is educational, not medical advice. Do not show blood, open wounds, or real crash victims.

## Voice-over script

**0:00–0:04 — Hook**
A seat belt does not stop your body. It buys your body time.

**0:04–0:16 — The second collision**
In a crash, the car stops first. You do not. For a moment, your body keeps moving at the speed you were travelling. That is why the injury often happens a beat later: when your body catches up with the inside of the car.

**0:16–0:30 — What the belt does**
The belt does two things. It keeps you inside the vehicle, because ejection is one of the deadliest events in a crash. And it lets you ride down the impact. The belt stretches slightly; force limiters let a little webbing spool out. More distance, more time, less peak force.

**0:30–0:38 — Hard points**
Luckily, your body has hard points: pelvis, rib cage, shoulder. Unluckily, the belt only uses them if it sits on them.

**0:38–0:47 — Ball analogy**
Think of catching a fast ball. Lock your arms and the impact stings. Pull your hands back and the force drops. A seat belt is that pull-back, for your whole body.

**0:47–0:55 — Fit**
So fit matters. Lap belt low across the hip bones, not the stomach. Shoulder belt across the chest and collarbone, away from the neck. Never under the arm or behind the back.

**0:55–1:02 — Callback and close**
The belt is not there to squeeze you tight. It is there to let you stop slowly.

Buckle up. Put the belt on bone. Educational only: follow your manual and local law.
